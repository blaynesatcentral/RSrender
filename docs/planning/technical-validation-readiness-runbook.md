# Technical validation environment readiness runbook

**Tickets:** Live evidence gates #30, #34, #36–#40, #42, and #43  
**Status:** Dependency-safe pre-provisioning plan after completion of #10/#16/#21/#32/#33 and #22–#27; owners, approvals, environments, and test results are not claimed  
**Scope:** Human authority, non-sensitive inventory, isolated environments, and handoffs needed to start later RSrender evidence tickets promptly  
**Non-claim:** This document records no owner appointment, environment approval, product decision, credential access, signing operation, participant observation, installation, or completed technical test.

## Purpose

The product, domain, UX, architecture, lifecycle, recovery, acceptance, and roadmap frontiers are decision-complete. #10 closed through explicit uncertainty acceptance and #16 provides a synthetic semantic corpus without representative labels. Remaining release and capability gates depend on firm-owned hardware, storage, security, deployment, records, accessibility, source evidence, and rights authority. Those organizational dependencies can be identified and scheduled without inventing results.

This runbook separates:

- **inventory now** — non-invasive, non-client-specific facts and owner availability that may be gathered before technical prerequisites close;
- **provision later** — isolated environments and inert test authority that may be prepared only through the firm's approved process; and
- **decide/test later** — measurements and policy decisions that remain owned by their GitHub prerequisites.

It supplements the [human participation plan](human-participation-plan.md). GitHub issue #1 and the live ticket bodies remain authoritative for dependency state.

## Shared authority and privacy boundary

Names, contact information, usernames, hostnames, serial numbers, tenant/account identifiers, storage paths, signing-key locations, certificate private material, security configurations that would increase operational risk, consent records, and internal policy text stay in approved non-public systems.

Repository/GitHub updates may contain only sanitized categories, version/build information approved for release, aggregate readiness states, test-fixture identifiers, measured results approved for publication, limitations, and decision outcomes.

Never place in a readiness packet:

- production credentials, cookies, refresh/access tokens, secrets, recovery keys, or authenticators;
- production RSLog requests/responses, tenant/source/entity identifiers, or undocumented endpoint traces;
- client data, production Log Projects, Source Snapshots, Supplemental Sources, Log Documents, or firm templates;
- signing private keys, production certificate material, feed credentials, internal management URLs, or unrestricted installer artifacts;
- real user/machine/storage paths, support bundles, logs, dumps, telemetry rows, or endpoint-security configuration details; or
- participant/observer identities, raw accessibility transcripts, recordings, or disability information.

Use independently created synthetic packages and, once approved, #16 fixtures. Every experimental machine, share, profile, account, and certificate path must be isolated and authorized for destructive/failure testing. This runbook grants no permission to interrupt production systems.

## Common readiness states

Use only these aggregate states in repository-visible tracking:

- `Owner not identified`
- `Owner identified; authority not approved`
- `Authority approved; inventory pending`
- `Inventory complete; dependency blocked`
- `Provisioning authorized and active`
- `Environment ready; dependency blocked`
- `Eligible for ticket execution`
- `Evidence collection active`
- `Decision/approval active`
- `Complete with limitations`

`Environment ready` never means the dependent prototype passed. `Inventory complete` never means a technology or policy was selected.

## #43 supported authentication/source evidence readiness

### Supported-authentication evidence request

Through an authorized vendor-supported route, request documentation or a value-free demonstration covering:

- interactive login method and whether browser/system SSO, MFA, device authorization, or organization policy applies;
- two-factor/challenge occurrence, continuation, cancel, expiry, retry, and recovery states;
- access-token and refresh/session lifecycle, expiry, refresh, revoke, sign-out, and invalidation;
- tenant/account selection and isolation, multiple accounts, account switching, and offboarding;
- minimum scopes/permissions for read-only RSLog access;
- proxy/offline/error behavior and supported retry/rate-limit guidance;
- whether a desktop/public client registration is supported and who owns its lifecycle; and
- test/sandbox account availability that contains no production client data.

Record source, date/version, support status, and limitations. Do not collect credentials, raw token values, production responses, or undocumented routes.

Grade each fact separately as `documented`, `vendor-confirmed`, `authorized-observed`, `inferred`, `failed`, or `unresolved`. A published route, successful response, returned shape, semantic match, vendor-support statement, firm permission, and credential-policy approval are different evidence states. Existing RSAgent code or captured application routes cannot establish vendor support.

### Firm integration questions that may be inventoried now

- whether the firm permits no persistence, OS-protected persistence, or both for approved applications;
- shared Windows profile/shared workstation policy;
- sign-out, revoke, rotation, loss, offboarding, profile change, uninstall, and incident ownership;
- approved local diagnostic/support evidence and required redaction;
- whether multiple RSLog accounts/tenants must coexist; and
- security review/approval route for inert-secret canaries and vault failure tests.

### Settled boundary and remaining gate

#21 selected the source-only adapter output boundary, and #32/ADR 0004 selected session-only authentication with no persisted RSLog secret. Those choices are closed. #43 may promote only vendor-supported, authorized source/authentication facts and clean-room value-free shapes; firm security must approve the concrete integration and evidence handling. Missing evidence keeps the affected integration unavailable and never authorizes `safeStorage`, Windows Credential Manager, credential persistence, or an undocumented route.

## #36 storage-durability readiness

### Storage-class inventory

The IT storage owner classifies only actual or proposed supported destinations:

| Class | Inventory questions | Required isolated capability |
|---|---|---|
| Local fixed NTFS | Supported endpoint/volume policy, long paths, ACL/read-only, quota, AV/EDR/backup interaction. | Process termination, VM reset where applicable, sharing locks, low-space/quota, permission changes. |
| SMB/network share | SMB/server class, offline/redirected behavior, cross-machine writers, reconnect/failover policy. | Isolated share and clients where failure/race tests cannot affect production. |
| Firm sync product | Product/mode category, placeholders, conflict copies, offline behavior, known-path policy. | Isolated test tenant/library/profile and permission to force sync races. |
| Removable/exFAT | Whether proposed for support; device/filesystem class. | Sacrificial media and reset/unplug permission. |
| Other proposed class | ReFS, mapped drives, redirected profiles, EFS, or other category only if actually proposed. | Explicit isolated environment and owner. |

Do not inventory user paths, share names, account names, client data, or production topology in the repository.

### Must wait

ADR 0003 and closed #33 select the package adapter/parser direction used by #36. Numeric retry/deadline limits and supported/degraded/rejected classifications come only from repeated #36 measurements. A local-NTFS prototype result does not generalize to SMB, sync, removable media, or power loss.

## #38 recovery-policy readiness

### Inventory policy ownership now

Identify who can decide:

- which Source Snapshot, Supplemental Source, Embedded Template Representation, Presentation Override, and asset categories may appear in Recovery Candidates;
- whether endpoint encryption or additional application encryption is required;
- permitted behavior on shared workstations/profiles and profile migration;
- retention/disposition authority, legal/records hold, administrative cleanup, uninstall, and incident handling;
- whether users may compare, open separately, retain, or discard candidates and which actions require audit; and
- what sanitized recovery status may enter diagnostics or support evidence.

Also record the security/privacy/records authorities who can approve or attest organizational use of the already-settled recovery policy after its named #36/#37/#39 mechanics evidence is available. An endpoint administrator, storage owner, or product owner cannot silently stand in for those approvals.

Use categorical inputs only. Do not record client content, paths, identities, raw recovery packages, or internal policy text in the repository.

### Settled boundary and remaining gate

The [recovery policy](specifications/recovery-retention-privacy-policy.md) settles numeric interval, maximum count/age/bytes, disk-pressure behavior, startup classification, cleanup eligibility, privacy, and user-visible commands. #36/#37/#39 must still provide the named mechanics evidence, and #38 must obtain the required organizational approvals/attestations. Readiness work must not reopen those defaults or the deliberate separate-opening/new-identity decision.

### Inventory now

| Area | Required readiness fact |
|---|---|

| Windows | Supported release/build and ability to reproduce clean profiles/configurations. |
| Electron | Ability to install the later pinned packaged build from #37. |

| Privacy/retention | Restricted location, access, retention/disposition, incident route, sanitization, and release approver. |
| Repeatability | Snapshot/reset instructions and environment/version manifest. |

The controlled manifest must pin the Windows build/patch, RSrender package hash/signature, Electron/Chromium/Node versions, process topology, Narrator/NVDA versions, conditional JAWS/braille disposition, inspection/capture tool versions, input/locale, display driver, Contrast Theme, Windows Text size, display scale, application zoom, canvas zoom, and network posture. Any relevant OS, application, runtime, AT, driver, fixture, task, or capture-tool change invalidates the affected qualification row until rechecked.

Before environment construction, #16 must hand off a sanitized fixture/task receipt containing a stable bundle/version, approved synthetic hashes, provenance and rights, zero-client/vendor-asset review, workload classification, Contents/page/accessibility-state coverage, allowed-use boundary, and deterministic reset oracle. #37 must hand off the installed packaged build, signature/hash/provenance, process/security profile, and reset/crash procedure. A development server, system browser, or unpackaged harness cannot substitute for either handoff.

### Must wait

#16 supplies approved synthetic tasks without representative labels. #37 must supply the pinned packaged Electron boundary. Only then may #40 prove the environment can execute the required tasks reproducibly. #40 does not prove RSrender accessible; directly observed #34 tasks and the final acceptance strategy's A-rows remain separate.

Simulated zoom, CSS forced colors, DOM/accessibility-tree inspection, or automated focus checks must be labeled simulation/inspection. They cannot substitute for actual OS configuration and human-observed AT behavior.

#40 is environment-complete only when Narrator and NVDA are actually executable and observable; JAWS/braille scope is explicit; actual required Contrast Themes, 200% Windows Text size, 100/125/150/200% display scales, and conditional mixed-monitor transitions are repeatable; sanitized tasks load/reset; authorized evidence channels can repeat focus/status/error/cancel smoke paths; and the environment record passes privacy approval. This proves environment readiness only and hands off to #34.

## Cross-ticket provisioning sequence

```text
Now
  -> identify owners and approval routes
  -> collect non-sensitive endpoint/storage/deployment/auth/source/rights/recovery/AT/resource-limit inventory
  -> reserve isolated resources and test windows, subject to authority

#10/#16/#21/#32/#33 and #22–#27 are complete
  -> final specifications, synthetic corpus, source/auth/package decisions, and acceptance methods are authoritative
  -> closed #41 authority/workload inputs permit #30 planning under its live ticket
  -> #36/#37/#39 can execute their storage/process/deployment evidence
  -> #43 can seek authorized source/auth/asset evidence and rights
  -> #42 can qualify resource limits

#36/#37/#39 evidence plus required authorities
  -> #38 can complete organizational recovery approval/attestation without changing settled defaults

#16 complete + #37 packaged boundary + #40 environment approval
  -> #40 can complete readiness evidence
  -> #34 can perform observed accessibility tasks
```

Parallel scheduling is encouraged; evidence and authority are not conflated. A shared IT discovery meeting may populate several inventories, but each ticket keeps its own prerequisite, experimental record, decision, and approval.
