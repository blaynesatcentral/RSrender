Human participation is therefore an explicit workstream in the goal process. Names and contact information belong only in approved non-public systems, never in this repository or GitHub.

### Wave 1 — representative workflow evidence

Run six core #10 sessions after authority, privacy, and consent are approved. Cover every required function and maximum-variation condition. Synthesize novelty and coverage before scheduling the saturation checks.

Run at least two later sessions. They count toward the stopping rule only when both occur after the minimum-six and functional-coverage conditions and add no new critical workflow, publication blocker, or fixture-changing edge case. Do not repeat a participant merely to manufacture saturation.

Custodians then approve anonymized counts, artifact-derived categories, and synthetic fixture revisions. The synthesis approver performs the final privacy review. Any unresolved representative claim is either kept open or receives an explicit product-owner uncertainty decision with downstream restrictions.

The smallest normally defensible plan is therefore eight individual sessions, not six sessions conducted all at once.

## Batching without conflating authority

- An IT/support owner may complete one #10 current-practice interview and a separately labeled authority segment for #36/#39/#40.
- A security/records owner may approve #10 research handling and separately approve/attest #38 only after the required technical evidence exists.
- An RSLog/data steward may join an optional #10 session and later #43 work under separate authorization; interview consent does not authorize technical access.
- An author, maintainer, or reviewer may later participate in #34 under a new task protocol and consent record.
- A reviewer who also has publication authority may supply both evidence and a policy decision, but the records must distinguish those roles.
- Product-owner decisions may be scheduled together administratively, but each issue retains its own prerequisite, answer, and confirmation record.

## Non-substitution rules

1. Management appointment is not participant consent.
2. Participant preference, majority count, or job title is not product policy or role authority.
3. Product-owner approval is not representative evidence, IT/security/legal approval, or vendor authorization.
4. A #10 interview cannot prove interaction usability, keyboard completion, screen-reader behavior, or conformance.
5. Automated DOM/UIA/accessibility-tree output cannot replace directly observed Narrator/NVDA/JAWS behavior.
6. #40 proves an environment is ready; it does not close #34 or declare RSrender accessible.
7. IT description of current practice does not select supported storage or updater technology; #36/#39 require measured evidence and authorization.
8. A preliminary recovery preference does not close #38; the settled recovery policy still requires its named #36/#37/#39 evidence and security/privacy/records approval.
9. Synthetic fixtures and prototype measurements do not prove representative frequency, professional acceptability, or user usability.
10. A single multi-function participant counts only for functions genuinely performed and evidenced.
11. #29's product-owner GO does not replace exact dependency, asset, employer, legal, signing, rollout, or commercialization review.
12. Research-evidence retention for #10/#40 and product Recovery Candidate retention for #38 are separate regimes; approval of one does not authorize the other.

## Repository and privacy boundary

The [#10 representative-validation protocol](research/internal-workflow-representative-validation-protocol.md) governs participant research. Before human collection, the repository may contain protocols, empty instruments, controlled schemas, synthetic fixtures/tasks, and non-claim readiness records.

After consent, custody, sanitization, human privacy review, and designated approval, the repository may contain only approved synthesized findings, approved aggregates with provenance and limitations, revised workflows/workload bands, ranked frequency/consequence results, approved synthetic fixture changes, contradiction/uncertainty summaries, sanitized environment/version matrices, approved policy tables, and closure attestations.

Never commit participant identities, contact details, consent records, raw notes, recordings, observer transcripts, raw speech/braille/UIA traces, participant-level metadata, identity mappings, production artifacts, client data, credentials, production API responses, full paths, or restricted policy records.
